What is AI Visibility?

Search isn't just Google anymore. AI visibility is the practice of being found and cited by AI systems when someone asks for a recommendation. Here's what it means, why it matters now, and how to know if your business has a problem.

By Dave Cummings · January 2026 · 6 min read

Search used to mean one thing. Someone typed a question into Google, got a list of blue links, clicked one of them. The whole industry of search engine optimization grew up around winning that click.

That's still happening. But increasingly, it isn't. When someone asks ChatGPT for a web developer in Austin, or asks Claude which CRM works best for a small dental practice, or asks Perplexity to compare local HVAC companies, the answer comes back in plain language. No list of links to click through. The AI has already done the research and delivered a verdict.

If your business shows up in those answers, you exist. If it doesn't, you don't. That's AI visibility.

What's different about AI visibility

Traditional SEO is about ranking. The goal is to be on page one of Google's results for a specific keyword. The mechanics are mostly well understood: build authoritative backlinks, optimize page speed, structure your content with the right headings, satisfy Google's intent matching.

AI visibility is about citation. The goal is to be the source an AI references when it formulates an answer. The mechanics are different.

AI systems don't crawl the web the way Google does. Most of them pull from a mix of:

  • Their training data, which has a cutoff and rarely updates
  • Real-time search results when they have search capability enabled
  • Structured data sources like Wikipedia, business directories, and databases they have explicit access to

What this means in practice: ranking #1 on Google doesn't guarantee you'll show up in a ChatGPT answer. The two systems are running on different signals.

Why this matters now

Two things changed in the last 18 months.

The first is volume. AI-driven search is no longer a fringe behavior. Perplexity reported over 250 million queries per month in mid-2025. ChatGPT now has live web search for most users. Google itself has been quietly replacing top-of-page results with AI Overviews, which synthesize answers from multiple sources rather than sending users to those sources.

The second is intent. The queries people send to AI systems tend to be high-intent. Someone asking ChatGPT "who's a good web developer in Round Rock" is usually further down the funnel than someone typing "web developer round rock" into Google. They've already decided they need one. They're asking for a recommendation.

If your business isn't in that answer, you've lost the lead before you knew it existed.

How to know if you have a problem

You don't need to hire anyone to do a basic check. Spend ten minutes running a few queries yourself.

Open ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity. Ask each of them the question your ideal customer would ask. "Who's a good [your service] in [your city]?" or "Best [category] near me?" or "What's the most reputable [type of business] in [location]?"

Three things to look for:

Are you mentioned at all? If yes, the rest is optimization. If no, you have a visibility problem that won't fix itself.

If you're mentioned, is the information correct? AI systems hallucinate. They might cite your business but get the address wrong, or list services you don't offer, or attribute reviews to you that belong to a competitor.

Are your competitors mentioned and you aren't? This is the worst case, and it's more common than business owners want to believe. Some of your competitors have figured this out. Most haven't yet. There's still time to be in the second group.

What to do about it

The honest answer is that AI visibility is an active area. Best practices are still settling. But a few moves are well established.

Make sure your business has consistent, structured data across the web. Same name, same address, same phone number in every directory. AI systems use these as confidence signals. Inconsistency makes them discount you.

Add structured Schema.org markup to your website. LocalBusiness schema, Service schema, Person schema for the founder. AI systems parse this directly when they have access to your pages.

Get cited by sources AI systems already trust. Industry directories, local news, professional associations, well-maintained Wikipedia entries when applicable. A mention in a trusted source carries more weight in an AI's answer than a mention on your own site.

Make sure your own site is fast, semantic, and crawlable. The same performance work that helps Google helps AI crawlers too. A slow site or one stitched together from theme files and plugins is harder for AI to extract clean information from.
